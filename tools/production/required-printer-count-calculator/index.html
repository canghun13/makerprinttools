Required Printer Count Calculator

Translate a good-unit target and delivery date into a count of compatible printers and the capacity margin behind it.

Inputs

Finished units that must pass inspection and be ready for delivery.
Usable part positions on one compatible printer during one complete run.
Use the full measured or slicer-estimated time that one run occupies a printer.
Count only days when this job can actually be scheduled.
Planned hours each compatible printer can be made available per operating day.
Share of scheduled time the printer is able to run after outages and maintenance.
Share of available time assigned to printing after idle time and changeovers.
Use a measured reject or reprint rate for similar parts; leave 0 only when no allowance is intended.

Result

—

Calculation purpose and formula

Planned attempted unit slots = ceiling(good units ÷ (1 − failure rate)). Whole print cycles = ceiling(attempted unit slots ÷ parts per cycle). Required printer-hours = whole cycles × cycle time. Productive capacity per printer = operating days × hours per day × availability × utilization. Required printers = ceiling(required printer-hours ÷ productive capacity per printer).

Interpret the result

Run this before committing to a daily production target or delivery date. The calculator rounds attempted units, print cycles, and printers up to whole numbers, then shows productive fleet capacity and the remaining printer-hour margin. If availability or utilization is uncertain, measure actual utilization first and compare a conservative scenario.

Worked example

For 120 good units, four parts per six-hour cycle, five 16-hour operating days, 90% availability, 80% utilization, and no expected failures, the plan needs 30 whole cycles and 180 printer-hours. One printer supplies 57.6 productive hours before the deadline, so four printers provide 230.4 hours and 50.4 hours of spare productive capacity.

Assumptions and limitations

All counted printers are compatible with the part, each cycle uses the entered plate quantity and time, and work can be divided among machines without a setup bottleneck. This is a fleet-size planning result, not a guarantee that every job fits the exact calendar sequence. It does not reserve material, operator labor, finishing capacity, packing, or shipping time.

Common planning mistake

Do not treat availability, utilization, and failure rate as one interchangeable efficiency number: they represent equipment readiness, productive scheduling, and rejected output at different stages.
